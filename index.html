Intel and classic rules

How the scam works

You get one human pilot and three bots. Roll, claim unowned regions, and charge rent when another pilot lands there. Declined leases go to public auction.

Own a color group to build evenly: four Astrahus, then one Keepstar. The Bank has 32 Astrahus and 12 Keepstars. Freeport Keepstar pays nothing. Banned for RMT sends you directly to Team Security; use a held card, pay 50M, or try doubles.

The rules baseline is Hasbro's US C1009 (2017). All 32 card effects retain their classic amounts and destinations. ISK is cosmetic: 1M on this board equals one classic dollar. Open Assets to build, sell, or mortgage; open Trade to negotiate. You can manage holdings during another pilot's turn by pausing the bots.

Board research

The board mixes Faction Warfare flashpoints with nullsec regions known for blocs, rental empires, invasions, or all three. It is satire, not a live sovereignty map.

Bot doctrine

The bots see the same board you do. They do not peek at future rolls. Yield Hunter chases high-traffic income. Set Breaker finishes its groups and blocks yours. Network Baron values jump bridges, utilities, and connected holdings. All three bid when you pass.

The bots forecast nearby rent, bid for set completion, rush three Astrahus per region, and hoard scarce houses. They negotiate trades, redeem mortgages, and stay banned late in the game when the board is expensive. These are researched heuristic opponents; they do not peek at the shuffled decks or future dice.
